For the Young Reader
and for anyone who remembers being one

My mother reading to me is my earliest memory of books. That sowed in me the need to learn so I could read by myself.

The fondest story I remember from childhood is O. Henry's The Last Leaf. I cannot recall how or when I read it, but I remember sitting quiet after I read it and thinking about it. That last leaf and its image in my head is the earliest memory of a lasting vivid imagery from reading.

From then I have read many short stories as a child. I loved them. Our school had a library period, for primary classes it was a collection of books randomly assigned to students. You read what you got. There I discovered fairy tales. At home, I discovered Indian mythology, Panchatantra collections and other Indian classics. I lost count of the number of times I read stories from the Mahabharata over the years.

I assumed as a child that my affinity lay in short stories. Novels I presumed I would not like. However one library session I was assigned a novel. Whether or not you wanted it, we had to read what was assigned and write a summary. It was Enid Blyton's Five on a Treasure Island, the first book of the Famous Five series. I started to read, sure that I was not going to like it. But very soon I realised that it was after all a short long story. I enjoyed it so much the length and number of pages went out the window. Next I picked more of her books. That began my exploration of novels.

Another similar assumption I had as a child was that if I saw the movie then the book was redundant information. How very wrong I was. I had watched the first Harry Potter film before I ever read the books. When I got the first part, I hesitated. When I began to read, what absolute pleasure. The best hours of my life. That removed another barrier from my brain. Reading was absolutely different from watching a movie. It was your own movie.

Soon I took a membership at Ernakulam public library. Books were expensive and buying every now and then wasn't a feasible option. There I met Michael Crichton. Emboldened by the realisation that a movie is not the same as a book, I took up Jurassic Park. Again a fond memory of my childhood, discovering dinosaurs and learning their names.

Every single Harry Potter book I read, I read because of the generosity of a friend who lent me the book no strings attached, not even a warning or a lecture about how to care for it. Lent for the whole summer, to read at my pleasure. My first own Harry Potter set I got at seventeen, demanded as my present for clearing high school decently enough. But none of them had the effect the borrowed books did. The final book was released when I was fifteen.

I always wonder if I would have been as kind as my friend to lend it so. I hope so. I have tried to be.

I read because neither the library nor my home set any rules about reading. Sometimes the book I got or read may not have been age-appropriate at all. But I read them anyway and nobody cared. I explored my tastes myself at my own time. I also allowed myself to indulge in the reading rules set by school, which dropped my presumptions. I did not hasten to move from one genre to another or one author to another, it occurred inevitably. The more I read, the more I wanted to explore.

Along the way there was Roald Dahl, Ruskin Bond, C.S. Lewis, Tolkien, Stephen Hawking, Jane Austen, Emily Brontë, Agatha Christie and countless more. I still read all of them. And as the saying goes, no man crosses the same river twice, for it is not the same man and it is not the same river.

Similarly, you never read the same book twice because you are not the same person and it is not the same book. And so I have read, re-read, again and again at various stages of my life.

Books have been my friends. I meet versions of myself in them. I started to curate my reading only recently but from three to thirty, through school, college and PhD days and today, the books were always there. Preserving the phases of my life between their pages.

Sulthana Shams

Hello,

I am a researcher by training. I love to read, discover and write, so I made that my profession. A day in my life involves a lot of thinking about things, then writing down some of the good ones. Then testing some of the ideas and waiting for results that sometimes arrive and sometimes don't. I learn for a living, which is one of my greatest joys. You can find my research here.

I also wrote a book once — my PhD thesis. You can read it here.

Somewhere along the way, books became the other place where I went to ask questions. I started recording what I read only recently. This library is that. Many books I have loved live only in memory now. The ones here are the ones I caught in time.

I read across most things, Indian literature, science, history, speculative fiction, whatever a generous friend or a library shelf puts in my path. I still believe a borrowed book is better than a bought one. I still believe reading the same book at different ages is like meeting an old friend who has grown in ways you hadn't expected.

If a book on this shelf made you pick it up, that is enough.

Dr. Sulthana Shams
